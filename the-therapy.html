The Therapy

Therapy that helps you understand the pattern and change it.

A practical, structured therapy that pays close attention to the loop between what you think, how you feel, what your body does, and what you end up doing about it.

§ I — What CBT can help with

Eight areas I work with regularly.

Fig. I Anxiety

Anxiety & overthinking

Racing thoughts that won't switch off. "What if" scenarios on loop. Physical tension you can't quite shake. Anxiety pulls you out of the present and into worry that, when you look at it honestly, is solving nothing — even though it feels productive.

We map the worry cycle: what triggers it, what keeps it going, and what you do to try to feel safe. From there we work on practical responses — thought challenging, behavioural experiments to test anxious predictions, and worry-time techniques to contain rumination.

Anxiety responds well to structured CBT. Most people notice meaningful shifts within the first six to eight sessions.

Fig. II Perfectionism

Perfectionism & high standards

High standards that started as motivation have become the thing in the way. Anything less than perfect feels like failure. Procrastination kicks in when you can't guarantee a perfect result — then a harsh inner critic arrives for procrastinating.

We examine the beliefs driving the standards and test whether they're actually helping you achieve more, or just making you miserable. Graded exposure to doing things "well enough," values clarification, and self-compassion practice.

We build flexibility without lowering your standards — the goal is high standards that serve you, not ones that hold you hostage.

Fig. III Health anxiety

Health anxiety

Constant worry about symptoms. Googling conditions late at night. Seeking reassurance from doctors, partners, or family — and the relief never quite lasting. Every physical sensation feels like a possible threat.

We map the cycle — worry, checking, brief relief, more worry — and work on tolerating uncertainty about health. That includes examining unhelpful thinking patterns and gradually reducing checking and reassurance behaviours.

Fig. IV OCD-spectrum

OCD-spectrum difficulties

Intrusive thoughts that feel dangerous or meaningful. Compulsions to check, wash, count, or correct until something feels "just right." Brief relief from the ritual — and then the cycle repeats.

The treatment with the strongest evidence base for OCD is Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP). We build it up gradually — deliberately stepping into the trigger and choosing not to do the compulsion, starting well within your tolerance.

Fig. V Performance

Performance pressure & exam stress

A presentation, exam, or interview on the horizon, and your mind fills with worst-case scenarios. Heart racing, mind going blank. In the run-up you avoid preparation because thinking about it feels too threatening.

Graded exposure to performance situations, cognitive restructuring to challenge catastrophic predictions, practical relaxation techniques, and confidence-building strategies that prepare you for the real thing.

Fig. VI Low mood

Low mood, burnout & motivation

Everything feels heavy and pointless. Activities that used to bring satisfaction now feel like obligations — or you've stopped doing them altogether. The drive is gone, and everything feels like too much effort.

We start with behavioural activation — reintroducing small, meaningful activities to break the withdrawal cycle, even before motivation returns. The principle is that action precedes motivation when low mood is involved.

Book a free consultation
Fig. VII Sleep

Insomnia & sleep difficulties

Your mind switches on the moment you get into bed. Going over the day, worrying about tomorrow. Or you fall asleep easily and wake at 3am with thoughts racing. Tired but wired.

CBT-I (CBT for Insomnia) addresses both the behaviours and the thoughts that perpetuate sleep problems. Sleep hygiene, stimulus control, relaxation training, and — if appropriate — sleep restriction, a counterintuitive but highly effective technique.

CBT-I is as effective as sleep medication without the side effects, and the improvements tend to last.

Fig. VIII Panic

Panic & physical anxiety symptoms

Heart pounding, breathing tight, a sudden sense that something terrible is about to happen. After the first panic attack, the fear of having another can become all-consuming — your world starts to shrink around the fear.

We start with understanding what's actually happening in your body during an attack, and why it's deeply uncomfortable but not dangerous. Then: interoceptive exposure, breathing retraining, and gradual exposure to avoided situations.

The aim is not to brace against panic but to lose the fear of it, which is what allows the panic itself to settle.

§ II — How CBT works

A model for what's happening, and a process for changing it.

Each element feeds the next. CBT identifies which loops are keeping you stuck and changes them.

1

We make sense of what's happening now.

The situations that trigger distress, the thoughts that show up, the feelings in your body, and what you do or avoid as a result. We map the pattern in detail.

2

We identify the cycle that's keeping it going.

Rather than asking only why something started, CBT asks what is maintaining it now. That gives us something concrete to work with from the early sessions.

3

We test new ways of responding.

Behavioural experiments, exposure work, thought records, problem-solving. Small changes that test the predictions anxiety and low mood quietly make.

4

You build skills you can keep using.

The aim is not to stay in therapy. It's to leave with a clearer understanding of your patterns, practical tools, and the confidence to use them on your own.

§ III — Inside a session

Sessions are collaborative, focused, and practical.

The first few minutes are a check-in: how the week has been, what came up, whether the things we agreed to try last time were possible. From there we set a focus for the session and work through it together. The format adapts; the structure underneath doesn't.

A clear focus for each session
Space to talk — but not just talking for its own sake
Practical tools to take into the week
A pace that feels manageable, not pushed
Regular reviews of whether therapy is helping

You don't need to have it all figured out.

Sessions are £80 for fifty minutes. The consultation is free, lasts fifteen minutes, and exists so we can both work out whether this is the right fit.

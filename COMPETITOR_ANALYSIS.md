## Competitive Analysis for Vision Wellbeing

### Industry Standards (features every competitor has — we MUST match)
- Clear session pricing visible on the site (range: £70-£85/session across competitors)
- Free initial consultation or assessment pathway
- BABCP or BACP accreditation prominently displayed
- Condition-specific pages explaining how CBT helps each issue
- Testimonials or outcome statistics
- FAQ section addressing "does online therapy work?" and logistics
- Privacy/confidentiality reassurance for online sessions
- Multiple contact methods (form, email, phone)

### Gaps (things competitors do poorly or are missing — our opportunity)
- **Design quality is universally poor** — ThinkCBT is text-wall heavy with dated design; OxfordCBT is WordPress template; AnxietyTherapist uses GoDaddy builder; Onebright is corporate but generic. None feel premium or hand-crafted.
- **No interactive tools** — No competitor offers free CBT exercises, thought records, or interactive toolkits. Jack's existing toolkit is a genuine differentiator.
- **No content marketing** — ThinkCBT has a downloadable workbook (good lead magnet). No competitor has a structured article library or courses. Jack's 20+ articles and 2 courses are unmatched.
- **No modern motion/animation** — All competitor sites are completely static. Scroll animations, page transitions, and micro-interactions will immediately set Vision Wellbeing apart.
- **Weak funnels** — Most competitors dump visitors on a services page and hope they book. No structured journey from awareness to conversion. OxfordCBT has a self-assessment quiz (good idea) but it's basic.
- **No block booking incentive** — Only Vision Wellbeing frames block booking as a therapeutic intervention ("pre-commitment is the intervention"), not just a discount.
- **Young people specialisation underserved** — Most competitors focus on adults only. Jack's dual specialism (adults + young people) is uncommon in the private online CBT market.

### Best Practices Spotted (things one or more competitors do well — consider adopting)
- **ThinkCBT: Free downloadable CBT workbook** — excellent lead magnet that builds trust and captures emails. Jack's courses (PAUSE, MAP) and toolkit serve this purpose even better.
- **OxfordCBT: Self-assessment quiz** — helps visitors self-qualify before booking. Consider a lightweight "Is CBT right for me?" interactive element.
- **Onebright: Outcome statistics** — "87% recovery rate" is a powerful trust signal. If Jack can share any outcome data, it would be compelling.
- **ThinkCBT: Extensive condition list** — having a page or section for every condition helps SEO. Jack's therapy page already does this with 8 detailed sections.
- **OxfordCBT: Therapist bios with photos** — humanises the team. Jack has this advantage as a solo practitioner — the personal connection is immediate.

### Content & Copy Patterns
- Common terminology/keywords: "evidence-based", "BABCP accredited", "structured", "online CBT", "cognitive behavioural therapy", "secure video", "confidential"
- Typical headline patterns: "[Condition] CBT", "Online CBT Therapy UK", "How CBT Works"
- How pricing is presented: Per-session rate prominently displayed, most show single price, few offer block discounts
- Trust signals: accreditation badges, university affiliations, years of experience, recovery rates, review counts

### Recommended Site Structure
Based on competitor analysis and client brief:
1. **Home** — PAS hero, credentials, specialism bento grid, how it works, testimonials, about, pricing snapshot, CTA
2. **The Therapy** — 8 treatment area deep-dives with anchor navigation, how CBT works model, inside a session
3. **Fees & Booking** — 3-tier pricing, embedded Setmore, contact form, FAQ, Edinburgh waitlist
4. **Resources** — articles grid, courses, toolkit link
5. **404** — branded error page

### Recommended Differentiators
Things we should include that NO competitor currently has:
1. **Premium design with motion** — GSAP scroll animations, Lenis smooth scroll, Barba page transitions, Vanta fog hero. No competitor has any of this.
2. **Interactive CBT toolkit** — free thought records and exercises. No competitor offers this.
3. **Structured content library** — 20+ articles as SEO funnel. ThinkCBT has a workbook but no ongoing content.
4. **Research credibility** — active NHS research involvement, Moray Endowment funding. No competitor leads with research.
5. **"Pre-commitment is the intervention" framing** — therapy-informed pricing that reframes block booking as part of the treatment, not a sales tactic.
